Disposal-intent coverage is partial: 4 of 48 banks. Those 4 carry a quote located in its source document by automated check. The remaining 44 are ranked on reported regulatory data alone and are shown as intent unconfirmed — they are not asserted to have said nothing. Treat the vertical axis below as a worked demonstration of the method, not as full market coverage.

1. Where the stock sits

Each bubble is a bank, sized by absolute NPL stock. The uncomfortable corner is upper-left: a large non-performing book that has not been provisioned for. A bank to the right of the dotted line can sell near carrying value; one to the left takes a P&L hit to transact, which is why it waits.

2. The rotation

Stage 2 loans are performing today but have seen a significant increase in credit risk — they are the pipeline of tomorrow's NPLs. Germany carries the highest Stage 2 share and the lowest coverage of the four systems, while Italy, the market most associated with NPL supply, is now the best provisioned.

3. Pressure against stated intent

Reported data measures what a balance sheet forces management to confront. It cannot measure what management has said it will do. The vertical axis is the language layer; where it is unpopulated, a bank is marked intent unconfirmed rather than assumed silent.
